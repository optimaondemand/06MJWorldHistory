Primary Source 2
The Black Obelisk of Shalmaneser III
About this source: D.D. Luckenbill's translation of the short epigraph (label caption) carved above the Black Obelisk's second relief register, erected at Nimrud around 825 BC. Source: Ancient Records of Assyria and Babylonia, Vol. I (1926), Section 590. Public domain. Like the Narmer Palette in Week 5, the genuine object carries only a short label caption, not an extended narrative, so no longer verbatim passage exists to quote.

The full epigraph accompanying the register showing Jehu's submission:
"The tribute of Jehu (Ia-u-a), son of Omri (Hu-um-ri); I received from him silver, gold, a golden saplu-bowl, a golden vase with a pointed bottom, golden tumblers, golden buckets, tin, a staff for a king, and spear-shafts."

A description of the accompanying carved image (an art-historical note, not a direct translation):
The register showing Jehu depicts him prostrate, face to the ground, before the Assyrian king Shalmaneser III, while a line of attendants behind him carries the tribute goods named in the epigraph. It is the only surviving ancient image identified as a specific, named Israelite or Judahite ruler.

📝 EDITORIAL NOTE ON THE INSCRIPTION'S LANGUAGE
Assyrian scribes routinely described a defeated or tribute-paying kingdom's ruling line using the name of an earlier, often more famous dynasty, in this case "the house of Omri" (a previous ruling family in Israel), even though Jehu himself had violently ended that specific dynasty. This is a useful reminder that ancient inscriptions sometimes preserve administrative habits or shorthand that do not perfectly match a kingdom's own internal political history.
