💡 ABOUT THIS SOURCE
Samuel Butler's prose translation of Homer's Odyssey, Book 9, quoted directly. Public domain; hosted in full at Wikisource, the same translator used for Week 8 and this week's Lesson 1 Iliad excerpt. Butler renders the Greek pun as "Noman" rather than "Nobody"; both are accepted English renderings of the same wordplay.
📖 ON THE FALSE NAME

"Cyclops, you ask my name and I will tell it you; give me, therefore, the present you promised me; my name is Noman; this is what my father and mother and my friends have always called me."

📖 ON THE ESCAPE AND THE OTHER CYCLOPES'S RESPONSE

When the other Cyclopes heard Polyphemus's cries and asked what ailed him, "Polyphemus shouted to them from inside the cave, 'Noman is killing me by fraud; no man is killing me by force.'" Hearing this, they went away unconcerned.

📖 ON ODYSSEUS'S FINAL, COSTLY BOAST

"Cyclops, if any one asks you who it was that put your eye out and spoiled your beauty, say it was the valiant warrior Ulysses, son of Laertes, who lives in Ithaca." Polyphemus then prayed to his father Poseidon: "grant that Ulysses may never reach his home alive; or if he must get back to his friends at last, let him do so late and in sore plight after losing all his men."

📝 EDITORIAL NOTE
This episode is often read as a pure celebration of cleverness, and in large part it is. But Homer includes Odysseus's final boast deliberately, showing that even his most cunning hero cannot always resist the pull of pride, and that this particular lapse carries a real and lasting cost for the rest of his journey. As with Achilles in Book 1, virtue and vice appear here in the very same person, not neatly separated into different characters.
