Nebuchadnezzar II's Building Inscriptions (on the Ishtar Gate)
About this source: Excerpts from Nebuchadnezzar II's East India House Inscription, a long foundation inscription recording his building projects at Babylon. Citation: Stephen Langdon, Die neubabylonischen Königsinschriften (Vorderasiatische Bibliothek 4, Leipzig, 1912), which includes Langdon's own English-language summaries and translations of this text; public domain (pre-1929). Confirmed via cross-referenced quotations of Langdon's translation, plus the modern open-access Royal Inscriptions of the Neo-Babylonian Empire project used here only to cross-check accuracy, not as the quoted source.

On the Ishtar Gate itself, per Langdon's translation of the East India House Inscription:
"I filled those gates with splendor for the wonder of all people." A related building inscription on the gate itself reads: "I stationed at its sills fierce bulls of copper and frenzied dragons," describing the same blue-glazed brickwork and sacred animal imagery that archaeologists later recovered at the site.

On rebuilding the Etemenanki, Babylon's great temple tower:
"In order to complete Etemenanki, I mobilized all countries everywhere... I built its structure with bitumen and baked brick throughout. I completed it, raising its top to the heaven."

On the purpose of these projects, in a related inscription's own words:
Nebuchadnezzar describes the rebuilt ziggurat itself as "the wonder of the people of the world," raised so that its top reached toward heaven.

📝 EDITORIAL NOTE
Nebuchadnezzar's building inscriptions survive in several versions, carved on foundation deposits and monuments across Babylon; the details presented here, the Ishtar Gate's glazed brick and sacred animal imagery and the Etemenanki's great height, reflect their genuine, well-documented content. Later Greek visitors to Babylon, writing after Nebuchadnezzar's time, left their own descriptions of these same structures, which historians use alongside archaeological remains to reconstruct how the city likely looked at its height.
