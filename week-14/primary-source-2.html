Primary Source 2
Herodotus's Account of the Battle of the Eclipse (585 BC)
About this source: G.C. Macaulay's 1890 translation of Herodotus's Histories, Book I, describing the war between Media and Lydia and the eclipse that helped end it. Public domain; confirmed via direct fetch of Project Gutenberg's hosted text (gutenberg.org/files/2707).

On the course of the war before the eclipse:
"[There was] war between the Lydians and the Medes lasting five years; in which years the Medes often discomfited the Lydians and the Lydians often discomfited the Medes." In the sixth year of fighting, "suddenly the day became night."

On the prediction of this event:
"This change of the day Thales the Milesian had foretold to the Ionians, laying down as a limit this very year in which the change took place."

On the armies' reaction and the resulting peace:
Both armies ceased fighting at once when they saw the day turn to night, and became eager to make peace. The two sides were reconciled through mediators who arranged "an interchange of marriages," with the Lydian king Alyattes giving his daughter Aryenis in marriage to the Median royal house, and the river Halys was set as the boundary between the two kingdoms.

📝 EDITORIAL NOTE
Modern astronomers, working backward from known eclipse patterns, generally agree a solar eclipse visible in this region did occur around 585 BC, lending some real support to this account, though historians still debate exactly how much credit Thales personally deserves for predicting it in advance.
