The Legendary Founding of Rome
Primary Source 2
The Legendary Founding of Rome (Romulus and Remus, per Livy)
Primary Source Reading
💡 ABOUT THIS SOURCE
Rev. Canon Roberts's 1912 English translation of Livy's Ab Urbe Condita ("From the Founding of the City"), Book 1, chapters 4-7, quoted directly. Public domain; hosted in full at Project Gutenberg (gutenberg.org/files/19725).
📖 ON THE TWINS' EXPOSURE IN THE FLOOD

Livy records that the newborn twins were exposed, cast into "the current of the river" at "the nearest land-flood, where now stands the ficus Ruminalis" (the fig tree later sacred to Rome).

📖 ON THE SHE-WOLF AND FAUSTULUS

"[A] thirsty she-wolf, coming from the neighbouring mountains, directed her course to the cries of the infants, and… held down her dugs to them with so much gentleness, that the keeper of the king's flock found her licking the boys with her tongue. It is said his name was Faustulus; and that they were carried by him to his homestead to be nursed by his wife Laurentia."

📖 ON THE BROTHERS' DISPUTE OVER THE SITE

"Romulus chose the Palatine and Remus the Aventine hill as their stands to make their observations. It is said, that to Remus an omen came first, six vultures; and now, the omen having been declared, when double the number presented itself to Romulus, his own party saluted each king," each brother's followers claiming the kingship for their own man.

📖 ON THE FOUNDING AND THE KILLING THAT FOLLOWED

"Remus, in derision of his brother, leaped over his new-built wall, and was, for that reason, slain by Romulus in a passion; who, after sharply chiding him, added words to this effect: 'So shall every one fare, who shall dare to leap over my fortifications.'"

📝 EDITORIAL NOTE
Livy wrote this account roughly seven centuries after the traditional founding date of 753 BC, drawing on generations of Roman legend rather than any contemporary record. Modern historians generally treat Romulus and Remus themselves as legendary rather than historical figures, though archaeological evidence does confirm that a genuine settlement existed on the Palatine Hill by roughly this period. Notice how closely the twins' river rescue echoes Sargon's basket story from Week 3, a pattern worth naming rather than treating as coincidence.
