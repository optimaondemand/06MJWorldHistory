Herodotus, Histories Book II (on Egypt)
About this source: G.C. Macaulay's 1890 English translation of Herodotus's Histories, Book II ("Euterpe"), written in the fifth century BC. Source: The History of Herodotus, translated by G. C. Macaulay (Macmillan, 1890), public domain, hosted in full at Project Gutenberg (gutenberg.org/files/2131). These excerpts are chosen deliberately to include both Herodotus's careful, plausible eyewitness reporting and his more credulous traveler's tales, so that the two can be compared directly.

On Egypt's wonders, as Herodotus opens his account:
"Of Egypt however I shall make my report at length, because it has wonders more in number than any other land, and works too it has to show as much as any land, which are beyond expression great."

On the flooding of the Nile (careful, reasoned reporting):
Herodotus reports that no Egyptian, Libyan, or Greek could give him a satisfactory account of why the Nile floods in summer, unlike other rivers, and rejects the theory that melting snow causes it. He offers his own explanation instead: "the Sun passing in his course by the upper parts of Libya... draws to himself the water, and having drawn it he drives it away to the upper parts."

On the preparation of the dead (careful, plausible reporting):
"First with the crooked iron tool they draw out the brain through the nostrils, extracting it partly thus and partly by pouring in drugs; and after this with a sharp stone of Ethiopia they make a cut along the side and take out the whole contents of the belly."

On a phoenix said to visit the temple of the sun (an openly doubted tall tale):
"There is also another sacred bird called the phoenix which I did not myself see except in painting, for in truth he comes to them very rarely, at intervals, as the people of Heliopolis say, of five hundred years; and these say that he comes regularly when his father dies; and if he be like the painting he is of this size and nature, that is to say, some of his feathers are of gold colour and others red, and in outline and size he is as nearly as possible like an eagle."

On winged serpents guarding frankincense trees (a claim Herodotus presents as his own firsthand proof):
"There is a region moreover in Arabia, situated nearly over against the city of Buto, to which place I came to inquire about the winged serpents: and when I came thither I saw bones of serpents and spines in quantity so great that it is impossible to make report of the number, and there were heaps of spines, some heaps large and others less large and others smaller still than these, and these heaps were many in number."
