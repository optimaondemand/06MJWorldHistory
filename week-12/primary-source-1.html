Primary Source 1
Ashurbanipal's Library Tablets
About this source: Two genuine translated texts: Ashurbanipal's own inscription describing his scribal education, and the famous curse colophon he had copied onto many of his library's tablets. The colophon translation is a long-published, widely-quoted classic English rendering (Victorian-era phrasing such as "insofar as it existeth" and "knoweth" is original to the translation, not modernized).

On his own education as a young prince (unusual for a Mesopotamian king):
"I, Ashurbanipal, within the palace, understood the wisdom of Nabu... I learned the craft of the sage Adapa, the secret, hidden lore, all of the scribal arts."

The colophon (ownership and curse label) attached to tablets in the collection:
"I have transcribed upon tablets the noble products of the work of the scribe which none of the kings who have gone before me had learned, together with the wisdom of Nabu insofar as it existeth [in writing]. I have arranged them in classes, I have revised them and I have placed them in my palace, that I, even I, the ruler who knoweth the light of Ashur, the king of the gods, may read them. Whosoever shall carry off this tablet, or shall inscribe his name on it, side by side with mine own, may Ashur and Belit overthrow him in wrath and anger, and may they destroy his name and posterity in the land."

On the library's contents, drawn from what has actually been recovered:
Among the many thousands of tablets recovered from Nineveh are a nearly complete copy of the Epic of Gilgamesh, astronomical and medical texts, dictionaries pairing Sumerian and Akkadian words, and administrative and legal records, an enormous cross-section of Mesopotamian scholarly knowledge gathered under a single royal roof.

📝 EDITORIAL NOTE
The library's clay tablets, unbaked when Nineveh's palace was destroyed by fire in 612 BC, were accidentally hardened by that same fire, a detail explored directly in this week's graded VR activity.
